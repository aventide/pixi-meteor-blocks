# Moon (D10)

## What was built

- **Tiles** (`public/assets/planets/moon/`): same 128×128 structure as Earth's tiles (dark outer edge, vertical body gradient, bright inner rim, top gloss band, white glyph with a dark outline and a 35% drop shadow). Each tile face also has moon rock detail: regolith speckle, 3 small craters (dark bowl, lit floor, bright lower rim), and pale glowing mineral veins ending in faceted crystals. These sit at the tile edges so the glyph stays clear. The craters are tinted with the tile's own color, so the four colors stay fully saturated against the grey world.
  - Red = rocket, blue = crescent moon, green = ringed planet, yellow = four-point star plus a small sparkle. Each one has a clearly different silhouette (tall, C-shaped, wide, pointed). Yellow uses a dark brown outline (`#7a3f00`) for contrast, like Earth's yellow.
  - Burned = dark basalt with dead craters, a hairline crack, pale dust drifts along the bottom, no glyph and no glow, so it reads as inactive.
  - The tiles were generated by a small one-off script (kept in the session scratchpad, not committed). The SVGs are the source of truth now.
- **Background** (`src/themes/moon.ts`): near-black sky (only a faint blue lift at the horizon). About 900 stars on desktop, split into 7 groups that twinkle out of phase, plus 14 bright stars with a halo and a faint cross that twinkle on their own. Earth hangs at the upper right, drawn with `earthTheme.mapIcon`. Grey regolith is drawn in 2 layers (a darker far ridge and a lighter near ground), each with low-angle crater rims and pebbles. The layers drift sideways at 2.5 and 6 px/s for parallax and wrap seamlessly (each layer is drawn 2 screens wide with a ridge function that repeats every screen width). Everything is drawn in `resize`; `update` only changes alpha and x. A seeded RNG means a resize redraws the same sky.
- **Trail:** `fire`, `[white, pale blue 0xd6ecff, blue 0x8cc2ff, orange 0xffbe6a, 0xff7a2a]`: a white-hot core, a cool blue body, and orange tips.
- **Map icon:** grey moon with maria, 5 rimmed craters, a soft glow, terminator shading (masked), a highlight and a faint rim.
- **Palette:** accent `0xa6d8ff` (cool Earthlight blue), panel `0x151925`, text `0xeef3ff`.

## Tuning

| Value               | Moon       | Earth | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------- | ---------- | ----- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| gravity             | 380 (0.4×) | 980   | Floaty but not sluggish. A block takes about 2.4 s to fall the full board (Earth: about 1.5 s), so blocks still settle quickly enough to read the board. Real lunar gravity (0.17×) made falling feel stuck.                                 |
| launchVelocity      | -950       | -1200 | Peak height = v²/2g, about 1190 reference px (Earth: about 735), above the 1080 board. Almost any launch reaches the top, slowing visibly near the top for a floaty hang. -1100 made every launch an automatic clear and the planet trivial. |
| launchDescentRate   | 110        | 200   | Groups that stall drift down at about half Earth's speed, which gives more time to re-ignite.                                                                                                                                                |
| popVelocity         | -6000      | -6000 | A pop is a control response, not physics. It stops on contact, so gravity barely affects it. Kept snappy.                                                                                                                                    |
| dropIntervalMs      | 500        | 700   | 1.4× Earth's spawn rate, to balance launches that clear so easily. With no input the board overruns in about 50–75 s.                                                                                                                        |
| launchedBlocksToWin | 90         | 40    | Launches nearly always leave the board, and chance matches launch on their own, so the count climbs much faster than on Earth.                                                                                                               |

### How I checked balance (headless Playwright, 1440×900)

- _Idle_ (no input): at 520 ms, the board overran in 50–70 s. At 500 ms, about 75 s with 28 launched by chance matches. At 600 ms, an idle run reached 58/60 by itself, which was far too easy, so I made spawns faster.
- _Random-swipe bot_ at 500 ms / goal 90: won 2 of 3 runs (83 s, 105 s) and lost 1 (68 s, 40/90). For comparison, the same bot on Earth reached only 16–24/40 in 120 s without losing. This bot favors low gravity because every random match launches clean, so a deliberate human player's experience will differ. Moon should be harder than Ocean and Desert and easier than Casino. That needs a human pass in D13 against the other planets' final numbers, which I couldn't see from this worktree.
- These trials ran several pages in parallel, so the timings are noisy.

## Couldn't verify / suggestions for shared code

- **Longer-lived trail particles:** the ticket asks for longer-lived fire particles to sell the low gravity, but particle life is set per style in `src/effects/trails.ts` (off limits). Suggestion: an optional `trail.life?: [number, number]` (or a `lifeScale`) on `PlanetTheme["trail"]`, read by `TrailSystem`. Moon would use about 1.5×.
- The fire style's orange tips fade to a slightly muddy brown on the black sky, because fire uses normal blending with fading alpha. An optional additive tail for dark skies would fix this (shared code).
- I didn't take a dedicated star-map or trip-complete screenshot. The map icon is the same Container shown on the intro card.
- Not playtested by a human; the floaty feel was judged from screenshots and numbers.
